### Key RV Power Needs

A typical RV needs:
- **Refrigerator**: 50–150W continuous
- **LED lighting**: 10–30W
- **Phone/laptop charging**: 15–65W
- **Water pump**: 100–300W (intermittent)
- **Fan/vent**: 20–50W
- **Total baseline**: 100–350W

A mid-range magnetic generator (200–500W) covers most of these needs when paired with a modest battery bank.

## Best Prime Movers for RV Use

### 1. Wind-Driven Magnetic Generator
- **Setup**: Mount a small wind turbine on the RV roof or tow-behind
- **Output**: 200–600W in moderate wind (10–20 mph)
- **Pros**: Continuous power in windy conditions, no fuel
- **Cons**: Needs open space, noisy wind turbine (not the generator)

### 2. Tire-Driven (Road-Driven) Generator
- **Setup**: Attach a roller to the generator, press against RV tire while driving
- **Output**: 100–300W while driving
- **Pros**: Charges while traveling, no extra equipment
- **Cons**: Only works when moving, adds tire wear

### 3. Manual/Crank Generator
- **Setup**: Hand crank or bicycle drive
- **Output**: 50–150W (human-powered)
- **Pros**: Complete control, no external power source
- **Cons**: Labor-intensive, limited output

### 4. Water-Driven (for parked RVs)
- **Setup**: Small water wheel in nearby stream
- **Output**: 200–500W (if near flowing water)
- **Pros**: Continuous, reliable power
- **Cons**: Requires water source

## Installation Tips for RVs

1. **Mount the generator** in a ventilated area — under the RV, in a storage bay, or on the roof
2. **Use flexible shaft coupling** to connect to the prime mover (reduces vibration)
3. **Add a charge controller** — essential for protecting your battery bank
4. **Wire to an inverter** — convert DC generator output to 120V AC for RV appliances
5. **Add a transfer switch** — switch between generator, shore power, and solar

## Noise Considerations

The magnetic generator itself is **completely silent** — no engine, no exhaust, no vibration. The only noise comes from your prime mover:
- **Wind turbine**: Moderate wind noise (similar to a ceiling fan)
- **Tire-driven**: Low rolling noise
- **Manual**: Human effort noise

For RV camping, this is significantly quieter than a propane generator that runs 50–70 dB — loud enough to disturb neighbors and make conversation difficult.
