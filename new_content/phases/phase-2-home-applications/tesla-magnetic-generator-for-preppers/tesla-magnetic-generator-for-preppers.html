The Prepper's Power Trinity

The most resilient emergency power setup combines three sources:

  1. Solar panels — Daytime, predictable, silent
  2. Magnetic generator — Night/low-sun, wind/water driven
  3. Lithium battery bank — Stores all excess for emergencies

This trinity provides:

  • Redundancy — If one fails, others compensate
  • Seasonal balance — Solar summer, wind winter
  • Decade-long operation — All components last 15+ years

Prepper Emergency Power Sizing

Level 1: Essential Backup (500W)

Power: Phone charging, LED lights, radio, small fridge

  • Generator: DIY bifilar pancake coil (100–200W)
  • Prime mover: Bicycle drive or small wind
  • Battery: 100Ah LiFePO4
  • Inverter: 500W pure sine wave
  • Cost: $400–$800

Level 2: Full Emergency (1,500W)

Power: Lights, fridge, well pump, TV, tools

  • Generator: Large DIY or PMG (500–1,500W)
  • Prime mover: Wind turbine or hydro
  • Battery: 200Ah LiFePO4 @ 48V
  • Inverter: 2,000W pure sine wave

Level 3: Whole House (3,000W+)

Power: Everything including heat, AC, well pump, workshop

  • Generator: Professional PMG (3,000–5,000W)
  • Prime mover: Wind + hydro hybrid
  • Battery: 400–600Ah LiFePO4 @ 48V
  • Inverter: 5,000W+ pure sine wave
  • Cost: $5,000–$15,000

Grid-Down Survival Scenarios

Scenario 1: Weeks-Long Outage

  • Battery autonomy: 2–3 days (Level 2 system)
  • Generator extends: Indefinitely with wind/water
  • Priority loads: Fridge, lights, water pump, comms
  • Result: Comfortable survival for any duration

Scenario 2: Months-Long Collapse

  • Food preservation: Fridge stays running
  • Water access: Well pump keeps working
  • Communication: Phone/radio charging continues
  • Workshop: Tools for repairs, food prep
  • Result: Full self-sufficiency

Scenario 3: Seasonal Isolation

  • Winter: Wind generator + battery bank
  • Summer: Solar + magnetic generator backup
  • Spring/Fall: Wind generator primary
  • Result: Year-round power regardless of season

Prepper-Specific Tips

  1. Start with Level 1 — Build the essential system first, expand later
  2. Store spare bearings — Critical replacement part
  3. Keep tools organized — Generator maintenance requires basic tools
  4. Learn to wind coils — Spare coils can be wound on-site
  5. Document wiring — Label everything; future-you will thank you
  6. Practice regularly — Run the system monthly to keep batteries healthy
