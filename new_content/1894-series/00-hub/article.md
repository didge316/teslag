**Standfirst.** This site is about building things. So we did the unglamorous part: we
obtained a full scan of the 1894 first edition of Nikola Tesla's biggest book, took the
text layer that comes with it, checked that layer against the page images, and read it.
Twelve articles come out of that work. Each one cites the page it came from, and each one
shows an engraving from that page at a size you can actually read.

## What this book is

![Title page of the 1894 first edition](/assets/tesla1894/scan-title-page.webp "The title page of the volume this series is built on. Scan of the 1894 first edition, front matter (scan file 9). Public domain.")

The volume is *The Inventions, Researches and Writings of Nikola Tesla, with Special
Reference to His Work in Polyphase Currents and High Potential Lighting*, edited by
Thomas Commerford Martin and published in New York by D. Van Nostrand in 1894. The
copyright page says it was entered according to Act of Congress in 1893 by T. C. Martin,
and names the printers, Press of McIlroy & Emmet, 36 Cortlandt St., which is why
library records disagree about the date. We say *printed 1894, entered 1893*, and cite
the front matter scans (files 9 and 10) when it matters.

Martin is not a footnote here. The title page describes him as editor of *The Electrical
Engineer* and a past-president of the American Institute of Electrical Engineers, and the
book opens with a dedication "to his countrymen in Eastern Europe" (front matter, scan
file 6). He had seen the motors running. He wrote, of the paper at the centre of the
whole story, that it "was written the night previous to the meeting, in pencil, very
hastily, and under the pressure just mentioned" (1894, p. 9).

That is the kind of sentence you only get from a primary source, and it is the reason
this series exists.

## Why a series built on one book

Most English-language writing about Tesla's high-frequency work paraphrases his lectures.
The paraphrase is fine; it is just three handoffs away from the man. We hold the scan,
the text layer and the page numbers, and we can put the original engraving next to the
paragraph it belongs to.

The promise is narrow and checkable: every claim carries a page number from an 1894 book,
and every article shows an engraving nobody else is showing.

We are not publishing the book itself. It is 35 MB of scans and it is already on the
internet, hosted by the Internet Archive under the item name `inventionsresear00martiala`.
Go there if you want the whole thing; we link it from every article.

## How we cite, and how we could be wrong

![Fig. 297, part of the World's Fair exhibit with the motor apparatus on a long table](/assets/tesla1894/plate-p478-fig297.webp "Fig. 297, the full-page plate facing printed p. 478. The book's own text says it 'shows a view of part of the exhibits containing the motor apparatus' (1894, p. 477). The photographer's credit, 'Electrical Engineer', is printed in the lower right. Public domain.")

Three rules, stated up front so you can check us.

### The page offset

The scan files are numbered from the first page of the PDF, not from
the printed page. In this volume the printed page number is always the scan file number
minus eighteen. A citation of "(1894, p. 486)" means the page a reader of the physical
book would turn to, and we work it out by subtracting eighteen from the file we opened.
Front matter has no printed numbers, so we cite those by scan file instead.

### The OCR

Machine reading of an 1894 page is not a transcript. Long-s renders as `s`,
the digit 8 sometimes renders as `S`, and running headers get glued into the middle of
sentences. We silently fix only the two mechanical artefacts, long-s and the S-for-8
digit swap, and mark anything else odd with `[sic]`. Every quotation is re-grepped from the text
layer at writing time and pinned to a page before it ships. If you spot a quote we got
wrong, tell us; it is the failure mode we worry about most.

### The captions are not trustworthy

The book's own figure captions were machine-read
too, and sometimes badly. One engraving in the World's Fair chapter came out of the OCR
captioned "Fig. 811" (1894, p. 484), a figure number that does not exist in a book
whose illustrations stop in the 300s. We look at every crop before we use it, and if the
picture does not match the paragraph, we replace the picture rather than rewrite the
caption.

### The word search

A count is a rule before it is a number, so every count in this series says which text it
was taken from and how the phrase was matched: whole word or substring, and whether running
heads, bare page numbers and short display lines were kept or dropped. In this volume that
distinction is not pedantry. The string 'magnetic generator' occurs five times over the page
text as it sits in the file, and four times once the running heads and display lines are
dropped, because one of the five is the title of a chapter. Both numbers are correct of the
text each was taken from, which is why neither is printed without saying which index it came
from. The method, the counts for every term this series has searched, and the printed page of
every hit are on [Tesla 1894 Word Search](tesla-1894-word-search.html).

## What the book does not say

We searched the whole volume the way you would with a printed book and a pencil. The phrases
"free energy", "free-energy", "perpetual" and "over unity" appear **zero times**, whole word
and as a substring, in both indexes. The string 'magnetic generator' occurs five times and
never once as a phrase standing on its own: every occurrence sits inside 'pyro-magnetic
generator' or 'pyromagnetic generator', a device driven by heat changes in magnetised steel,
discussed at (1894, p. 429) and indexed at (1894, p. 496). Two of the five are running text,
one is the title of chapter XXXVI, one is the editors' preface listing what the volume
contains, and one is the book's own index entry.

That is a fact about an 1894 book, not a verdict on anyone's buying decision. But it is
the fact we started from, and the full word-search method is written up at
[Tesla's 1894 word search](tesla-1894-word-search.html).

## If you came here for the generator

The 1894 book never uses the phrase "Tesla magnetic generator", and nothing in this
series claims a magnet motor will run your house. What the book does contain is a man
describing machines he had built: motors at 94 and 95 per cent efficiency, an alternator
at 30,000 alternations a second, an oscillator that tore a steel shaft in half.

If you want the practical end of this, start with
