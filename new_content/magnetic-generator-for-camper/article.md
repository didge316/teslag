# Magnetic Generator for Camper: RV and Trailer Power (2026)

Whether you're in a travel trailer, fifth wheel, or Class B camper, a magnetic generator provides silent, fuel-free power for your RV lifestyle. No noisy gen-set, no propane, no hookups needed.

Here's how to power your camper with a magnetic generator.

## Best Camper Generator Setup

### Slide-Out Wind Turbine

- **Turbine:** 200W–400W, mounts to RV roof or slide-out
- **Generator:** Axial flux PMG integrated
- **Battery:** Upgrade existing lithium bank to 200–400Ah
- **Inverter:** Existing inverter (usually adequate)
- **Output:** 100–300W while parked in wind
- **Cost:** $500–$1,500

### Portable Magnetic Generator

- **Generator:** DIY bifilar pancake coil
- **Prime mover:** Bicycle drive or hand crank
- **Battery:** Portable power station or existing lithium bank
- **Best for:** Boondocking, short trips
- **Cost:** $200–$600

## Camper vs. Van Life

| Factor | Camper/RV | Van Life |
|--------|----------|---------|
| Space | More room for batteries | Tight constraints |
| Weight tolerance | Higher | Critical |
| Roof mounting | Easy (flat roof) | Limited |
| Existing inverter | Usually has one | May need to add |
| Battery bank | Already present (upgrade) | Need to install |

## Camper-Specific Tips

1. **Upgrade to lithium** — Most RVs have lead-acid; swap to LiFePO4
2. **Keep turbine stowed** — Drive with turbine folded or detached
3. **Use existing wiring** — Connect to RV's DC system
4. **Add a charge controller** — Between generator and RV battery
5. **Solar complement** — Keep roof panels; generator supplements

## Conclusion

A magnetic generator is a great supplement or replacement for RV gen-sets. Combined with existing solar and lithium batteries, it eliminates propane and fuel costs.
