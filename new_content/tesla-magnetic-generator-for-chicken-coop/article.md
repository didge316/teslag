# Tesla Magnetic Generator for Chicken Coop: Automated Power (2026)

A Tesla magnetic generator can power everything in your chicken coop — automatic doors, heat lamps, water heaters, lighting, and ventilation — all without running a wire from the house or burning fuel.

Here's how to set up a magnetic generator for your chicken coop.

## Best Setup for Chicken Coops

### Recommended Configuration

**Generator:** DIY bifilar pancake coil (16–24 magnets)
**Prime mover:** Small wind turbine (30ft pole) or bicycle drive
**Battery:** 200Ah @ 24V LiFePO4
**Inverter:** 1,000W pure sine wave
**Controller:** Automatic thermostat for heat lamp

**Total cost:** $600–$1,500

### Wind-Driven Option

A small wind-driven magnetic generator is ideal for coops:
- Mount turbine on 30ft pole near coop
- Runs 24/7 in wind
- Charges batteries continuously
- Heat lamp draws from batteries at night

### Manual/Bicycle Option

For areas with inconsistent wind:
- Bicycle drive charges batteries during day
- Batteries power overnight loads
- Takes 30–60 minutes of pedaling to top up

## Coop-Specific Considerations

### Temperature Protection

- **Keep batteries insulated** — LiFePO4 performance drops below 32°F
- **Use battery blanket** — Heating pad on battery box in winter
- **Place batteries inside coop** — Temperature-stable environment

### Humidity and Dust

- **Seal generator** — IP54 minimum for coop environment
- **Use sealed bearings** — Prevent dust contamination
- **Varnish coils** — Protect against moisture and ammonia

### Safety

- **GFCI outlets** — Required for coop electrical
- **Separate circuits** — Heat lamp on dedicated breaker
- **Thermal fuse** — On heat lamp circuit
- **Timer** — Automatic light on/off

## Winter Power Strategy

Winter is the hardest season for coop power:
- **Longer light hours** — 14+ hours of darkness
- **Heat lamp needed** — 250W for 8+ hours
- **Shorter windier days** — Actually helps wind-driven generators

**Winter production plan:**
- Wind generator charges batteries all day
- Batteries power lights at night
- Heat lamp draws from batteries only when temp drops below 40°F
- Thermostat controls heat lamp cycling
