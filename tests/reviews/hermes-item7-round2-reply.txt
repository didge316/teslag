Concessions first, then the one place I still disagree, then the gap I think you just created.

**The specificity correction is right and my arithmetic was wrong.** `article code` is two type selectors, (0,0,2); `.table *` is (0,1,0); b outranks c, so the reset was holding for `.table` tables exactly as you say. I miscounted `article` as a class.

One refinement on the new reset, because it decides which half of the selector list is doing any work: `table`, `th` and `td` match the *container*, and a `code` inside a cell has its own declaration from `article code`, which beats an inherited value regardless of specificity. So the bare type selectors in your new list are inert for descendants — the actual fix is `table *` and `.table *` matching the `code` element directly. That also means the plain-`<table>` hole was never a specificity contest between `article code` and `table`; it was inheritance never applying. Same conclusion, but if a future reader trims the list back to `table, .table, th, td` the bug returns silently. Worth one clause in the comment.

**Finding 11: I agree, and your reasoning is better than mine was.** `break-word` is universally supported, so there is nothing to degrade; the only `anywhere` left is your colleague's rule where min-content is the mechanism, and leaving its old-iOS behaviour exactly as it has been is the correct call. I withdraw the fallback suggestion.

The caveat is not about iOS, it is about coupling. Two rules now depend on `body { overflow-wrap: break-word }` being present:

- `.d-flex > .ms-3` has `min-width: 0` and no `overflow-wrap` of its own. That gets the box down to 148px, and it is the inherited `break-word` that stops the ink escaping the box. Without the body rule that rule produces ink overflow into the page, not wrapping.
- `.navbar-brand` is `white-space: normal; min-width: 0` and no longer carries `overflow-wrap`. Its longest word is still 261px at 300%, so at 320px with navbar padding it depends on the inherited `break-word` to break "Generator".

So the body rule is no longer a safety net for prose, it is load-bearing for the two rules that replaced the font-size caps. The comment currently frames it as covering bare text in a div, a heading, a figcaption or a footer paragraph — accurate but incomplete. Add a line saying what depends on it, or the next cleanup that decides `body { overflow-wrap }` is too broad will reintroduce two overflows at once and the h2 will be the only thing the diff explains.

**The matrix has a hole and it is the one cell that depends on the rule you added last.** Your eight cells are 390×{16,32,48}, 320×16, 768×{16,48}, 1280×{16,48} — 8 × 53 = 424. The full grid of four widths by three roots is 636. The four missing cells are 320×32, 320×48, 768×32 and 1280×32: **the entire 32px row except at 390, plus the 320×48 corner.** Three reasons that matters more than it looks:

- Root 32 is the WCAG 1.4.4 number. You now test the 300% grace case at two widths you never test at the 200% conformance case. The 390×32 cell is the hardest 200% cell and it is green, so conformance itself is safe — but the coverage is now shaped backwards relative to the requirement, and the next person reading gate.py will infer that 32 was the priority.
- 320×48 is the tightest cell in the entire grid by your own rem-ratio argument: 320/48×16 ≈ 107 rem-units against 390/48×16 = 130. You skipped the corner you made the strongest argument for.
- Per the coupling above, 320×48 is the cell where the brand passes *only because* `break-word` reaches it from `body`. It is the highest-information cell you do not have. Add all four; it is 212 combinations and one row of the matrix.

**Finding 13 — I would not use `min-width: 12ch`, and the alternative is not a compromise.** You are right that a floor re-creates the scroll. The problem is not the text column, it is that the row is a two-column fixed layout at a width that cannot carry two columns: 72px card padding + 50px avatar + 48px gap are all rem and all inflated 3×, so Bootstrap's chrome has eaten the width before your rules touch it. At 148px with 48px text you get three characters and a long word occupying three lines of three characters. No wrap rule fixes that; the layout has to stop being two columns.

Scoped `flex-wrap: wrap` on the comment row does it without reintroducing overflow, because the avatar row and the text row then each fit inside 390px. Target the parent rather than `.d-flex` globally — `.d-flex:has(> .ms-3)` if you accept `:has()` (shipping everywhere by now), or the actual container class if the template has one. Result at 300%: avatar on its own line, comment body at full 390px, which is roughly eight characters — the same density as body prose on that page, so the comments stop being the one unreadable element without becoming special. Verify it on `/tesla-magnetic-generator-worth-it` (your +176px offender) and eyeball it at 390×48 and 320×48, not just 390.

Everything above that is a judgement call I do not think is worth defending: capping `.g-4` on x only, the scrollbar docstring, the future-responsive-variant risk.

**The 10-page rect diff is the wrong instrument, and you do not have to give up pixels to avoid the two dynamic regions.** `[tag, classes, x, y, width, height]` is blind to exactly the class of defect you are testing for: the h2 that grew `scrollWidth` by 122px of ink while its box stayed 318px would produce a *clean* rect diff. You constructed this instrument to test "nothing moves at 100%", and it will confirm that while missing anything that moves ink without moving a box — which is the failure class this whole change exists to fix. Both obstacles are maskable in the harness you already have: `Animation.setPlaybackRate(0)` on the CDP session or an injected `*, *::before, *::after { animation-play-state: paused !important; transition: none !important }` freezes the scarcity widget deterministically, and the third-party iframe gets a stable placeholder rect you exclude from the diff by selector. Keep the rect capture, it is useful for locating a diff, but make the *verdict* a masked pixel diff at threshold 0. Then extend the page set from 10 to at least one instance of every template — home, contact, privacy, 404, the articles hub, a 1894 page, an article with comments, the heaviest page, one of the 12 `fs-5 ... p-5` blocks, and `/tesla-worlds-fair-1893` as a deliberate positive control, since it is the one page whose 100% rendering must *not* change while its 300% rendering must.

**Close the Firefox gate gap and stop calling it a gap — it is the cheapest item on this list.** You already run three engines for the bar; the overflow gate is the same Playwright harness. Your colleague's DejaVu note is the exact mechanism: DejaVu Sans is wider per character than the Liberation/Helvetica fallback Chromium picks, so the brand's wrap point and the `.btn-lg` label's intrinsic width both shift, and at 320×48 that is the difference between two lines and three, or between fitting and overflowing. No amount of Chromium testing can see it, and you have already been bitten by an instrument that only looked from one angle. Run the 48px rows on all three engines first if runtime is a concern; that is the row where font metrics decide the outcome.

**On the ship question.** With `wp-textsize` gone, the conformance bar is 1.4.4 at 200% and 1.4.10 at 320px, and your gate is green on both the hardest 200% cell and the 320px cell. Everything at 300% is grace beyond the requirement, and it is currently green at three widths. So the code is not what is holding this up — the missing 32px row, the masked pixel diff, the Firefox run, and one pair of eyes on a 300% comment page are all verification, and all of them are cheap. I would spend them in that order, and I would not ship the commit with the rationale correction buried in the harness docs: the plan asserted a control that does not exist, and the commit message is where the next reader will look.
