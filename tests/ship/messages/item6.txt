Item 6: the 32-item "Pages" dropdown becomes four top links and three themed groups

The nav was, in DOM order: brand, Articles, Home, Contact, About, and one dropdown
called "Pages" holding **32 links** - the whole site behind a click, on every page,
with the hub, the tool and the commercial pages buried inside it. It is now:

  brand · Articles · Home · Contact · About · **Build & plans**▾ · **Power your home**▾ · **Free energy & Tesla**▾

Four top links, unchanged in text, order and position; three groups of exactly **10,
10, 10** sitting exactly where "Pages" sat. That leaves the row's reading order
identical to what regulars have, and changes only what was buried.

**Where the 32 went: 30 into the groups, 2 out of the nav, by name.**
`apply_nav.py --check` prints the accounting and fails on anything unexplained,
because the check the plan describes ("every destination in the nav resolves to a
page") cannot catch a destination that was *dropped* - a deleted href is not in the
nav to be wrong about. So coverage is computed against `HEAD`: 36 destinations in the
old nav, 34 in the new one, and the two that left are

  /renewable-energy-using-magnets-a-magnetic-revolution
  /unleashing-the-magnet-magic-is-free-energy-possible

named in the source as `HUB_ONLY`, and `check_hub_links()` refuses to let the rewrite
run unless each of them has a card on articles.html - which they do. They are not
orphans: nav/inventory.py reports "pages reachable ONLY through the nav: 0" both
before and after, and every dropdown href resolves. (That check is also where I
fooled myself today, from the other side: a grep for `href="/<slug>"` said those two
articles had *no* inbound body link at all. articles.html links them as
`https://teslamagneticgenerator.com/<slug>`, the absolute form the hub was written
with - the same trap inventory.py's own docstring warns about, approached backwards.
The check that ships counts same-domain absolute hrefs; my grep did not, and the
grep's answer would have been acted on if the tool had not already been right.)

Both demotions are also what keeps every dropdown at or under 10: the two articles
would otherwise be the 11th and 12th member of the only group they fit, and the
group's own rule is the definition of done here.

Two things about a 52-page nav rewrite are easy to get wrong and both were measured
rather than asserted.

**The tool, and what it is not allowed to touch.** apply_nav.py rebuilds the nav
region from one template, and its page set is **the pages git knows about**: it
prints `pages: 52 tracked by git; NOT touching 1 untracked (somebody else's work in
progress): electrical-experimenter.html` on every run. That page is an untracked
restore of a page deleted in c3232e0, part of work in flight. The discipline was
learned at 19:37 today, when a *different* tool of mine - item 5's whitespace
repair - globbed `*.html` and rewrote all 53 files including theirs; the edit was
provably meaning-preserving (the (tag, attribute, value) triple check) which is thin
comfort for the blast radius being wrong, so anything that bulk-edits pages in this
branch now refuses to touch what git does not track, unless explicitly told to.

Canonical means **idempotent per page**, not byte-identical across the site:
about.html is the one page carrying `nav-link active`, and a check that compares it
to the shared block reports the site's correct page as wrong and exits 1. Regenerating
each page's block from its own active hrefs is the invariant that matters - run the
tool twice, get the same file - and 52 of 52 pass it. verify.py asserts each dropdown
is <=10 items and that no real link href appears twice (the brand's `->/` excepted).

**The drawer.** Bootstrap's dropdown menu is white-space: nowrap, so at 390px the
long labels were clipped by the viewport edge; the first pass was `white-space:
normal` plus 0.75rem block padding for 44px touch targets. That fixed 16px and
nearly everything else about the drawer - and it was still broken at 200% and 300%,
which is what item 7 exists to prevent, discovered only when verify.py grew FONTS
(16, 32, 48) and re-ran every geometry section at each. Three more causes had to be
measured out, each invisible to the previous one:

  1. `min-width: 0; max-width: 100%` on `.navbar-nav .dropdown-menu` - Bootstrap
     5.2.3 floors a dropdown menu at 10rem, and 10rem at a 48px root is a 480px
     menu inside a 390px drawer while every item inside it was already wrapping
     with min-width 0. Everything item 6 had fixed was fine and irrelevant.
  2. `min-width: 0` up the chain (`li.nav-item`, `.navbar-nav`, `.navbar-collapse`) -
     max-width: 100% resolves against a flex item that is itself sized by the child
     being capped, so the drawer stayed 448px wide and the document reported
     scrollWidth 484 in a 390px viewport.
  3. `white-space: normal` on `.navbar-nav .nav-link` - the document still scrolled
     by 94px with the menu now comfortably inside, and what held it open was the
     *label of the button that opens the menu*: Bootstrap's CDN stylesheet declares
     `.dropdown-toggle { white-space: nowrap }` (confirmed by walking
     document.styleSheets for the rules that actually match the element rather than
     remembered from framework docs), and the three group toggles were the only
     items in the drawer it applied to - 448px of unwrappable label in a 318px box.
     A rectangle scan for "anything past the viewport" found zero offenders while
     the page scrolled; the scan that found it compares element.scrollWidth with
     element.clientWidth, which is where text overflow lives.

All three are inert at 16px by measurement: the longest toggle label is 190px inside
the same 318px box, so wrapping is never asked for at 100%.

**The interaction with item 7.** The restructure put the site straight back into the
failure item 7 had just closed: at 1280px with a 32px root font the nav row grew to
2068px inside a 1140px container and every page scrolled sideways again, +942px at
48px. `flex-wrap: wrap` on the row and `min-width: 0` on its links, scoped to
`min-width: 992px`, are what closes it: at 16px the brand takes 218px and the row
686px against a 960px container at the 992px breakpoint and 1140px at 1280px, so no
line is ever full and wrapping cannot trigger. verify.py re-measures exactly that at
992, 1024 and 1280 at all three root fonts, and requires one row at 100% (a nav that
wraps at 16px is a regression this change introduced) while only requiring "never
overflows" above it.

**Open dropdowns are measured too, because the overflow gate cannot see them.** The
matrix measures closed menus, and an absolutely positioned menu running past the
right edge does not move documentElement.scrollWidth either (measured: menu right
edges 1335, 1403 and 1512px against a 1280px viewport with scrollWidth reporting
1280). At a 32px root the three menus were 574/521/478px wide and the third starts
at x=825, so the last ~200px of every label sat off screen with no scrollbar to
reach it. Letting labels wrap and capping the box at min(28rem, 40vw) puts all of
them inside at the widths measured here - verify.py now opens all three menus at 992
and 1280 at 32/48px and asserts it, 24 configurations, red before those two rules.
What that does not cover is anchor positions and widths nobody has measured, because
nothing in CSS knows where the toggle sits; the structural fix is Popper's viewport
boundary, already shipped inside bootstrap.bundle, and that is a JS change to the
nav so it stays an owner decision. The check prints PASS-EXTRA today and
FAIL-KNOWN, excluded from the exit code, if it ever stops passing.

The root font is injected into the navigation response and read back off the page
before anything is asserted (tests/overflow/gate.py documents why the other three
ways of doing it do not work). The inventory numbers say something worth saying out
loud: five byte-distinct navblocks were in the tree, which is why a single
hand-authored replacement would have been wrong, and the tool is kept because a
52-page rewrite that exists only as a diff cannot be re-derived when the next page is
added tomorrow.
